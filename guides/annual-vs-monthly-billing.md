Almost every subscription app offers two prices: monthly, or annual with a discount. Many people pick annual because "two months free" sounds like a win. Whether it is depends on one thing: how likely you are to still be using the app in month twelve.

## The break-even month

Divide the annual price by the monthly price. The result is the number of months after which annual becomes cheaper than paying monthly.

| Monthly price | Annual price | Break-even | Annual saving if you stay 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10 | $96 | 9.6 months | $24 (20%) |
| $12 | $99 | 8.3 months | $45 (31%) |
| $15 | $150 | 10 months | $30 (17%) |
| $8 | $60 | 7.5 months | $36 (38%) |

If you stop using the app before the break-even month, monthly would have been cheaper. Our [annual vs monthly calculator](../tools/annual-vs-monthly.html) does this for any price and adds your own estimate of how likely you are to keep using it.

## Factor in honest probability

Ask yourself: what is the chance I still use this in a year? Be realistic. Fitness, language-learning and productivity apps are famous for enthusiastic starts and quiet abandonment. If you would put the chance at 60%, the expected value of the annual plan falls accordingly. A good rule:

- **Choose annual** for tools you have already used for at least two or three months, or that are part of your work.
- **Choose monthly** for anything new, seasonal, or tied to a goal you have not yet built a habit around.

## Try monthly, then switch

The safest pattern: start monthly, use the app for two or three cycles, then switch to annual if it has earned its place. You lose a little of the discount in exchange for not betting on a product you have not tested. Many apps let you switch plans at any time, and some apply your remaining monthly balance.

## Watch the renewal

Annual plans renew automatically, and introductory annual prices may not apply at renewal. Put a reminder in your calendar a few days before renewal so the decision is active rather than automatic. On iPhone and Android, the subscription screen shows the next renewal date.

## When annual is clearly right

- Software you use daily for work, especially if your employer reimburses it.
- Services you have used continuously for a year or more.
- Black Friday or seasonal offers on tools you were already going to renew. Check our [sale calendar](../calendar.html) for timing.

## When monthly is clearly right

- New apps, however promising.
- Seasonal use, such as tax software, a travel app or a sport you play for part of the year. Subscribe for the months you need, then cancel.
- Anything where a free tier or free alternative might do the job; check [free alternatives](../alternatives.html) first.

## The bottom line

The annual discount is a payment for your commitment. Only make that commitment for apps that have already proved they belong in your routine.
