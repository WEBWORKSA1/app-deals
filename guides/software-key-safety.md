The cheapest way to get software is sometimes the most expensive. Revoked licences, malware bundled with "free" downloads and fake stores that take payment and deliver nothing are all common. A few checks keep you on the safe side.

## Buy from the source when you can

The developer's own website, the official app stores and the developer's listed authorised resellers are the safest places to buy. For apps on phones and tablets, the official stores also give you a central place to manage subscriptions and request refunds.

## How to check an unfamiliar seller

1. **Is the seller listed by the developer?** Many developers publish a list of authorised resellers.
2. **Is the price plausible?** A key priced far below every legitimate sale is a warning sign.
3. **Does the site look like a clone?** Check the domain carefully for misspellings, extra words or unusual endings.
4. **Is there a real company behind it?** Look for a registered business name, contact details and a returns policy.
5. **What do independent reviews say?** Search the seller's name with words such as "scam" or "revoked key".

## Pay in a way you can dispute

Card payments and established payment services give you a route to dispute a charge if nothing arrives. Bank transfers, gift cards and cryptocurrency usually do not. A seller who insists on those methods for a software key is a red flag.

## Never download "cracked" software

Pirated software is a common carrier for malware, including password stealers and ransomware. Beyond the legal issue, the security risk is far larger than the cost of the software. If price is the problem, there is almost always a legal free route: an [open-source alternative](../alternatives.html), a free tier, an education programme or a library app.

## Phishing "deal" emails and ads

Fake deal emails often impersonate well-known software brands and link to look-alike checkout pages. Instead of clicking, type the vendor's address yourself or use a bookmark, and look for the deal there. If it is not on the official site, treat it as suspicious.

## Browser extensions that "find coupons"

Some coupon extensions are legitimate; others collect browsing data or inject affiliate codes. Install only well-known extensions, check the permissions they request, and remove ones you no longer use.

## After you buy

- Save the receipt and licence key somewhere safe, such as your password manager.
- Register the product with the developer if that is offered; it helps with support and transfers.
- Put the refund deadline in your calendar.

## Quick safety checklist

| Check | Safe sign |
|---|---|
| Seller | Developer, official store or listed reseller |
| Price | Close to known sale prices |
| Domain | Exactly the developer's domain |
| Payment | Card or established payment service |
| Download | From the developer or official store only |

If a deal fails any line, skip it. There will be another deal.
