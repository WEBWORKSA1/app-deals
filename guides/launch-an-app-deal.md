A well-run deal can bring an app its first thousand users, a burst of reviews and useful feedback. A badly run one brings support tickets from customers who will never pay full price. The difference is in choosing the right kind of deal and setting it up with your costs in mind.

## Choose the deal type

| Deal type | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Launch discount (for example 30-50% off the first year) | New apps that want paying users and reviews | Make the renewal price clear |
| Temporary price drop on a paid app | Paid mobile and desktop apps | Store ranking spikes are short |
| Free for a limited time | Paid apps seeking reviews and visibility | Expect many downloads, few conversions |
| Extended trial | Subscription apps with strong retention | Trial users need onboarding |
| Lifetime deal | Products with low marginal cost per user | Ongoing server and support costs |
| Education or nonprofit programme | Tools that benefit from habit formation | Verification overhead |

## Know your marginal cost

Before offering anything long-term, calculate what one additional user costs you per month in servers, third-party APIs, email, storage and support. If your product calls paid AI models or sends SMS, that number can be significant. A lifetime plan must cover years of that cost from one payment, so set usage limits on lifetime tiers that keep each customer profitable.

## Protect your pricing

- **Make the regular price public** before the deal, so the discount is honest and verifiable.
- **Limit the deal by time, not fake scarcity.** A published end date builds trust; resetting timers destroy it.
- **Keep deal tiers separate** from your main plans so you can evolve your pricing without breaking promises.
- **Write the terms in plain language**: what is included, what limits apply, and what happens with future features.

## Timing

- **Launch week** gives the strongest story: new product, early-adopter price.
- **Seasonal sales** like Black Friday reach the most buyers, but competition is intense. See the [sale calendar](../calendar.html).
- **Major updates** are a natural moment for a price drop on paid apps.
- **App store rules** apply to price changes and promotional offers. Use the store's own tools for introductory offers and promotional pricing where available.

## Prepare for the spike

1. **Onboarding:** a short welcome email and an in-app checklist that gets new users to the "aha" moment quickly.
2. **Support:** a help centre with answers to the ten most likely questions, written before launch.
3. **Feedback loop:** a simple way to collect feature requests and bug reports.
4. **Reviews:** ask satisfied users for a review after they have succeeded with the product, not on day one.

## Turning deal buyers into long-term customers

- Track activation, not just sales. A buyer who never activates will not review, refer or renew.
- Offer a fair upgrade path to the full plan for deal users who outgrow their limits.
- Communicate the roadmap. Deal buyers who see steady progress become your loudest advocates.
